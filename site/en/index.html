Marketing in Korea, explained

5 articles · 66 official documents archived · every quote checked on every build · no numbers without a source

If you are bringing a product or a shop to Korea, most of what you know about search and social media still applies. Three things do not. Naver comes before Google.

Reviews feed the search engine directly. And the law asks for a few things before you sell. This guide covers those differences, using official documents wherever we could get them.

About this site

An independent guide for businesses entering Korea. We sell nothing and collect nothing. Every claim rests on the platforms' own documents or the statute text; Korean quotes are verbatim and checked against our archive on every build.

Where we could not find a source, we say so.

Where Koreans actually search

Numbers here carry the survey, the year and who was asked. Without those three, a number is not worth repeating. All of these are secondary sources, so we say "reportedly". B

ServiceUse rate (Opensurvey 2026, 2,000 people aged 10 to 59)

Gemini28.9%

Naver and YouTube are reportedly trending down and the AI services up. The direction is set. But today, eight in ten of your customers are still on Naver.

A plan that starts with Google and Instagram will miss most of them.

Generative AI use reportedly reached 44.5% of the whole population in 2025 (Ministry of Science and ICT, 50,750 people aged 3 and over). Among internet users it was 87.4% in 2026 (KT Nasmedia, 2,000 people aged 15 to 69). Both are correct.

They asked different people. Keep the sample next to the number.
