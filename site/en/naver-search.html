How Naver search works, and what it removes

When a Korean customer types "bakery near me" into Naver, the page that comes back is not a list of ten blue links. It is four stacked blocks, and only one of them can be bought. This page walks through the four blocks, then quotes what Naver says it rewards and what it removes.

Published 2026-09-11Updated 2026-09-11Evidence: A Naver and Google documents4 min read

The four blocks

  1. 1
    Power Link. Paid, marked "AD", billed per click. Stop paying and it disappears.
  2. 2
    Place. Free. Built from your shop registration, photos, hours and reviews. For a local business this is the block that matters.
  3. 3
    Popular posts. Free. Blog posts land here. Naver's blog platform is where most Korean consumer writing lives.
  4. 4
    AI Briefing. Naver's own AI summary, built from the posts and reviews in blocks 2 and 3.

Blocks 2 to 4 cannot be bought. Getting into them without paying is what Koreans mean by search optimisation. It is less about keywords than you may expect, because Naver has written down what it removes.

What Naver says it removes

The quotes below are verbatim Korean from Naver's help documents, followed by our English rendering. A

Naver, "Blog search exposure restriction criteria", first line of the recommendations 본인이 직접 체험한 내용을 진정성 있게 작성하고
Our rendering: "Write sincerely about what you personally experienced."
Same document, reasons for non-exposure 실제 체험 없이 작성된 비체험 원고 게시물
Our rendering: "Posts written as commissioned copy without real experience."

This is the single most important line for a foreign brand. Agency-written posts that describe a product the writer never used are a stated reason for removal. Local blogger programmes exist, but the disclosure rules are strict and repeated sponsored reviews can drag down a blog's ordinary posts as well.

Naver, "Web content spam cases", keyword repetition 제목이나 본문에 동일 키워드를 의도적으로 반복하거나, 실제 명칭 대신 검색량이 많은 키워드로 치환하여 작성하는 경우
Our rendering: "Deliberately repeating the same keyword in title or body, or replacing the real name with a higher-volume keyword."
Naver, "Blog post exposure guide" 따라서 '상위 고정 노출'이라는 개념은 존재하지 않으며, 기존에 노출되던 게시물도 시점에 따라 순위가 변경되거나 검색 결과에서 제외될 수 있습니다.
Our rendering: "There is no such thing as a fixed top position; a post that was showing can drop or be excluded over time."

Any vendor promising a guaranteed top position is contradicting Naver's own text.

Google says the same thing

Google's Korean help centre document on helpful content contains the same two ideas: first-hand experience, and no preferred word count. A

Google, "Creating helpful, reliable, people-first content" (Korean edition) Google은 특별히 선호하는 단어 수가 없습니다.
Our rendering: "Google has no particular preferred word count."

What this means for a foreign brand

  • Korean-language content by someone who has used the product beats translated copy. Naver says so in writing.
  • Your Naver Place listing is the free block local customers see first. Set it up before any paid campaign. See Naver Place.
  • Reviews are not only reputation. Naver uses review text to match search queries. That is covered on the Place page too.
  • An AI-answer strategy (sometimes sold as GEO or AEO) only works on pages that search already finds. There is no reliable evidence for a separate technique. See our Korean page on AI answers.
